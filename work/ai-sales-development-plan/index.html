What it does

A complete AI plan for a sales-development team, delivered as a pack with two working demos, written for an interview task at a B2B software company.

Why I built it

The task asked how AI could help a sales-development team. Working demos with automatic checks show that better than slides, and testing the research first meant the plan didn't rest on an untested idea.

My role and the AI's role

  • Me: the scope, the choice to build working demos, commissioning the audits, and deciding what to cut.
  • Claude Code and Codex: built the demos, ran the research and the audits.
  • ChatGPT: prepared one version of the pack, which was then reviewed.

How it works

  1. Account brief. Reads the public company register for a target company and builds a one-page brief. Before any network call it refuses a company that's already a customer, and it refuses dissolved, dormant or inexact matches. A checker fails any brief whose fact has no source and date, or whose number doesn't appear in a cited fact.
  2. Call to handover. Turns a discovery-call transcript into CRM fields that each quote their evidence, a check against the team's qualification bar, and a handover note for the account executive. A checker requires every quote to appear word for word in the call and every number to have been said on it. The test call was fictional, with deliberate traps, and five more scripted calls were keyed by a separate AI agent.

Both demos are published as skills: account-brief and call-handover.

What I caught and changed

  • A 14-lens skeptical audit. Each finding was re-checked by a second agent told to try to refute it. Of 154 findings, none was refuted: 58 were confirmed and 96 partly true, ranked 9 critical, 52 major and 93 minor.
  • A second round on the final pack found 81 issues (26 wrong, 55 overstated) and no wrong numbers.
  • A cross-model check. When ChatGPT prepared a version of the pack, a review caught two lines that overstated their sources.

How it was checked

I had the plan's key idea tested before relying on it: a pre-registered backtest of whether public signals came before a purchase. It was inconclusive. With 4 datable buyers, the smallest p-value was 0.49, the power was about 5 to 11%, and about 31 buyers and 83 comparison companies would be needed for a fair test. The pack says that plainly, and dated predictions were written down before their outcomes are known.

Where it is now

Submitted for the interview. The company and its materials stay unnamed here.

Limits

  • The demos' answer keys were written in the same session as the demos, and the checker doesn't test whether a quote actually supports its field.
  • As of 16 Oct, nobody had run either demo end to end in a live setting.
  • The interview outcome isn't recorded here.
