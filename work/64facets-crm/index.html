What it does

64Facets was moving from a custom-built CRM to a new one, and the data for about 900 pieces had to be re-created in the new system. I was entering it by hand, at 3 to 4 hours a day, and it was clearly going to take months.

The automation pulled each piece's data from the old CRM, cleaned and reformatted it, and entered it into the new CRM. It handled a substantial portion of the records, not all of them.

It was part of a wider change: bringing AI-assisted steps into the team's everyday work, described below.

Why I built it

I was the one doing the re-entry, at 3 to 4 hours a day, and at that pace the move would have taken months. The data included client details I wasn't willing to send to a cloud service, so I needed a way to automate it that kept everything in-house.

My role and the AI's role

  • Me: spotted the problem, made the business case to the CEO, chose the approach, checked the output, kept the CEO and team updated, wrote the guides and taught colleagues.
  • Claude and Codex: helped build the automation.
  • A local model: did most of the entry work.
  • Our contract programmer: confirmed with me that new AI tools fitted the existing systems before they went into use.

How it works

  • Raise it early. I told the CEO that the manual transfer would take months, before it became a deadline problem.
  • Weigh it as a business case. The time saved compared with entering it by hand at 3 to 4 hours a day, and a local model compared with a cloud service on privacy and cost.
  • Keep the data local. The piece and client data were sensitive, so most of the entry was done by a 70-billion-parameter open-weight model running locally through llama.cpp. Claude and Codex helped build the tooling around it.

Bringing AI into the team

The migration was part of a wider change. With the team I mapped four processes (orders, wholesale, reporting and outreach), then introduced templates, workflow rules and AI-assisted steps one process at a time. I wrote short quick-start guides for each step and taught colleagues in small sessions, with the CEO separately. The CEO agreed the steps saved time across all four processes. There's no measured figure, so I don't give one.

Before a new AI tool went into use, I checked with the CEO or our contract programmer that it fitted the existing systems.

What I caught and changed

When I found an AI tool reading sensitive information it didn't need, I stopped that process, rewrote its master prompt and settings, moved the work to a local model, and told colleagues which data must never go into AI tools.

How it was checked

I spot-checked the early output by hand before trusting the automation, then checked the results against the old system. I don't have a record of the exact checking method or an error count, so I don't claim one.
