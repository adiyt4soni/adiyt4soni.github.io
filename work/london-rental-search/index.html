London rental search

Moving to London from abroad, I needed a room near the City without viewing dozens in person. I had Claude build a search system that collects listings, checks commute times and area data from public sources, has AI agents review every listing's photos against a written rubric, and ranks what's left. I chose from a shortlist and approved every message.

What it does

  1. Collect. Pulls listings from the rental sites I chose, and logs every one it sees.
  2. Commute. Asks the TfL journey planner for the door-to-door time to Bank in the City, arriving at 08:30.
  3. Area. Counts serious crime within 800 m of each station from police open data, and looks up the Census 2021 share of 25 to 34 year olds.
  4. Photo review. AI reviewer agents score every listing's photos against a written rubric: bed size, room size, whether a desk fits, light, how modern it is, the bathroom, and red flags.
  5. Rank. A written formula combines commute, room score, modernity, a private bathroom and price, with a penalty for each red flag.
  6. Shortlist. A board shows what passes every test. I pick, and nothing is sent to a landlord until I approve that exact message.

Mistakes caught

  • An early area run had matched the wrong station (West Hampstead instead of West Ham). It was caught and re-run.
  • An all-crime figure had been quoted as serious crime. It was corrected, and the method now states its definition of serious crime.

Both are now part of the area check's controls: a known hotspot and a known quiet area must separate, or the run isn't trusted.

Limits

  • The AI photo scores were never checked against human ratings, so their accuracy is unknown.
  • Commute times come from each site's approximate map pin, not the exact address.
  • Crime counts are by report location and lag by about two months; busy areas read high. It's a comparison tool, not a safety guarantee.

Tools and credits

  • Built with Claude Code (Anthropic). Python standard library.
  • Open data: TfL Journey Planner, data.police.uk, Nomis (Census 2021), postcodes.io.
  • The area method is published as a skill: uk-area-safety-screen.
